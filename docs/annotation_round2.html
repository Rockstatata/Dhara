01 Why we are doing this again

Not because the first round was careless — because of how the sheets were built.

Round one handed you sheets with the answer column already filled in by a machine pass. They came back unchanged. Across 170 questions that two people both annotated, not one answer differed — which is what happens when a pre-filled sheet is returned as-is. So we cannot say what those labels are worth, and we cannot measure agreement between annotators at all.

We did check the machine's answers against the advocate's published reply for the 330 questions that have one: roughly 48% look right, 24% look wrong, and the rest are unclear. So this is not a claim that the old labels are bad. It is that nobody knows, and every number in the project is measured against them.

This time the answer column starts empty. What you write is the label.

02 Three rules

  • Do not use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any AI to choose the answer

    This is the one rule that cannot be bent. Our central claim is that our retrieval system finds the right law. If an AI picked the labels, we would only be measuring how well our system agrees with another AI — and the whole result becomes worthless. Use the candidate list, the search tool, and your own reading.

  • Do not compare sheets with each other

    Some questions deliberately appear in two people's files. If you discuss them, the agreement figure we compute from them measures your conversation instead of your judgement.

  • Do not look at the round-one sheets

    Ignore annotate_*.csv entirely. If you happen to remember a question, judge it fresh anyway.

Unsure is a valid answer. Record low confidence and write a note. An honest “not sure” is far more useful to us than a confident guess — the confidence column is data, not a grade.

04 The answer column

Write either the candidate numbers or the chunk ids — both work.

  • 3Candidate 3 answers the question
  • 3, 7Two sections together answer it
  • labour_952_s340A chunk id you found yourself
  • noneYou could not find the answer, in the list or by searching
  • unanswerableNo law answers this question

none and unanswerable are not the same. none means the answer probably exists in Bangladeshi law but you could not find it. unanswerable means there is nothing to find — the person wants a lawyer's opinion, asks a factual question, asks about something outside our corpus, or the text is not really a question. Only unanswerable teaches the system when to stay silent, so mixing the two corrupts a separate measurement.

At most three sections. If more seem relevant, pick the three that most directly answer the question.

What counts as “the answer”

The section a lawyer would cite when answering this specific question.

  • Prefer the operative section over a definition. For “what is the punishment for taking dowry”, the answer is the penalty section, not the section defining dowry.
  • Prefer the section that answers what was asked. If someone asks what punishment their parents face for marrying them off young, the answer is the parents' punishment section — not the general prohibition, and not the registrar's duty, even though both are about child marriage.
  • If the question genuinely has two parts (“can I do this, and what if I don't”), two sections are right.
  • Do not add a section only because it is about the same topic. A near-miss recorded as correct hurts us more than a none.

Where you found it

  • candidateYou picked from the ten shown
  • own_searchYou found it yourself with the search tool
  • noneYou did not find an answer

This column becomes a real number in the paper: how often the candidate list simply did not contain the answer. Please keep it accurate.

05 When the candidates do not contain the answer

This happens often, and it is not your fault — it is one of the things the project set out to measure.

About a third of our corpus is English-only: the Succession Act, the Registration Act, the Code of Criminal Procedure and others. A Bangla keyword search cannot reach them at all, so for many questions the ten candidates cannot contain the right answer.

python scripts/07_search_corpus.py --query "ভরণপোষণ" --k 10      # search by words
python scripts/07_search_corpus.py --query "maintenance of wife"  # English works too
python scripts/07_search_corpus.py --act "Succession"             # list an Act's sections
python scripts/07_search_corpus.py --cite family_305_s10          # look up one section
  • If you know which Act applies, use --act and read down its section titles. Usually faster than guessing keywords.
  • For English Acts, search in English — the text is English, so Bangla words will not match.
  • Copy the chunk id (e.g. family_305_s10) into answer and set answer_source to own_search.

If you search and still cannot find it, none is the correct answer. Do not stretch to fit a candidate.

07 When you are done

  1. Save as CSV, UTF-8 with the same filename. In Excel: File → Save As → CSV UTF-8 (Comma delimited). In Google Sheets: File → Download → Comma-separated values.
  2. Do not add, remove or reorder columns or rows.
  3. Put the file in data/annotation/verified_round2/, or send it to Sarwad.

What your work produces

  • These 200 questions are the project's frozen test set — every result in the paper is measured on them. After this round every one of those labels is human-checked rather than machine-guessed.
  • Comparing your answers against what the machine proposed in round one gives us the accuracy of the old labels.
  • The questions two people annotated give us a real inter-annotator agreement (κ) — the number reviewers ask for first, and the one we currently cannot report at all.
  • Wrong labels get corrected, and the evaluation set stops being an unknown.
