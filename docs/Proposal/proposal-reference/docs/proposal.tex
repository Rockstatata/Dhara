\section{Introduction}
Computational work on social-media distress is largely organised around \emph{explicit} disclosure. Models trained on such data inherit a lexical shortcut: they learn that terms like ``suicide'' or ``kill myself'' predict the positive class, and this shortcut fails in two directions. Distress is frequently expressed \emph{implicitly} --- through hopelessness, perceived burdensomeness or farewell-like phrasing --- with no crisis vocabulary at all; and crisis vocabulary appears abundantly in text expressing no personal crisis, such as news commentary, third-party concern and hyperbole.

Compare ``I want to kill myself'' (explicit) with ``Everyone would be better off without me'' (implicit: no crisis term occurs, and meaning depends on composition in context). A bag-of-words representation can only encode the first; contextual transformer representations should, in principle, encode the second.

\textbf{Research question.} Can transformer-based NLP models identify crisis-related language expressed implicitly, when explicit suicide-related terminology is absent?

\textbf{Scope.} A text-classification research prototype, not a clinical diagnostic system; outputs classify language, not people.

\section{Research Gap}
The gap addressed is an evaluation and annotation gap, not a claim of novel subject matter.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Labelling provenance.} Widely used corpora derive labels from subreddit membership rather than post content, introducing label noise invisible in aggregate accuracy.
\item \textbf{Explicit dominance.} Where positives come from disclosure-norm communities, explicit phrasing is over-represented, so a model can score highly while learning little beyond a keyword list.
\item \textbf{Absence of hard negatives.} Negatives are usually topically unrelated, so the boundary is never tested against crisis vocabulary lacking personal crisis.
\item \textbf{No four-way organisation.} No surveyed dataset separates Explicit / Implicit / Hard Negative / Non-Crisis, so these slices cannot be reported independently.
\end{itemize}

Our own corpora confirm this. In the IRF splits (3,522 posts), 73.2\% of risk-labelled posts contain none of the markers \textit{suicid*, kill myself, end my life}; in SDCNL (1,895 posts), 62.8\% of positives contain no such marker while 18.4\% of negatives do. Implicit and hard-negative material is already latent here, but is neither separately labelled nor evaluable.

\section{Proposed Contribution}
\begin{itemize}
\item A manually annotated evaluation layer over existing public corpora distinguishing \textbf{Explicit Crisis}, \textbf{Implicit Crisis}, \textbf{Hard Negative} and \textbf{Non-Crisis} --- the primary contribution.
\item A controlled comparison of TF-IDF + Logistic Regression against a fine-tuned BERT classifier on identical data and an identical unseen test set.
\item Disaggregated evaluation reporting explicit, implicit and hard-negative cases separately rather than as one pooled score.
\item A qualitative error analysis of where contextual representation helps and which hard negatives induce false positives.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em, itemsep=1pt, topsep=1.5pt, parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Collect candidates.} Sample the corpora above, over-sampling posts with risk or causal annotations but no explicit vocabulary, and posts with crisis vocabulary but a negative label.
\item \textbf{Human annotation.} Each post receives exactly one of the four labels under written guidelines; a portion is double-annotated so inter-annotator agreement can be reported.
\item \textbf{Gold dataset.} Annotated items form the gold set; a target of $\sim$2,000 posts is indicative, not finalised.
\item \textbf{Split.} Partition into train/validation and a held-out test set, untouched during tuning and stratified so all three slices are represented.
\item \textbf{Models.} A baseline of TF-IDF features (word and character n-grams) with Logistic Regression, and a pre-trained BERT fine-tuned on the same data --- both tuned on the same validation split.
\item \textbf{Evaluation.} Both models on the identical test set: accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and confusion matrices overall, and --- as the primary result --- computed separately over explicit, implicit and hard-negative cases.
\item \textbf{Error analysis.} Examine items where TF-IDF succeeds and BERT fails, the reverse, where both fail, and hard negatives causing false positives.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ethical Considerations}
All data is drawn from existing publicly released research corpora with author identifiers removed; no user is identified, profiled or contacted, no clinical diagnosis is performed, and the system is never deployed on live user data.

\section{Limitations}
Subreddit-derived labels are noisy candidate signals rather than ground truth; the gold dataset is small relative to corpora typically used for fine-tuning; the implicit / non-crisis boundary is subjective, so annotator disagreement is expected and will be reported rather than concealed; and findings are limited to English social-media text, where classification performance is not clinical validity.
